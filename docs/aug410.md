Header: Part One: Budgeting 101 \- The First Pass. This is a first budget, not the final one. It's the rough draft that starts everything: the script is done, and it's time to start producing. Personal notes by Guadalupe Lareo.  
Phase 1 \- Read your script for money:  
1\. Make Three Lists: Read the script with three sheets of paper. Sheet one: every location, one tick per scene. Sheet two: every character, one tick per scene. Sheet three: everything that obviously costs money (cars, effects, stunts, songs, etc.).  
2\. Turn Pages Into Days: A small crew shoots three to four script pages a day. So a 90-page script usually means around 25 shoot days, plus location scouting, prep, and weeks of editing.  
Phase 2 \- Make the calls, then set up:  
3\. Answer Your Own Questions: Are you paying people, deferring, or asking for favours? Building sets or finding real places? Which camera and format? Who feeds everyone? Write each answer down.  
4\. Download a Template (Save Time\!): A ready-made film budget sheet already lists the hundreds of things you'd forget. Don't rename its line items and don't overwrite its formulas. If you need a line that isn't there, type into a blank one in the right section.  
Phase 3 \- The first budget is the worst-case scenario:  
5\. Price the Scary Things First: Go to sheet three from step 1\. Night scenes, stunts, effects, period costumes, crowds, far-away locations, etc. Price them now, while you can still rewrite them.  
6\. Do a Deliberately Fat First Pass: Budget the best version you might shoot, and assume you pay full price for everything. No favours unless they are already promised. Where you don't know a price, guess high and keep a list of what to look up.  
7\. Check That You Included This: Food for every person every day. Insurance. Hard drives and backups. Editing, sound mix, colour, music rights. Festival fees. Parking, petrol, permits. And a contingency line of around ten per cent, because something will go wrong.

8\. Write Your Assumptions Down: One page of plain sentences: "crew is unpaid", "the house is my parents", "no money for colour grading". In six months you won't remember why a number is what it is, and anyone giving you money will ask.  
9\. Save It, Then Make a Cheaper Version: Keep the fat version as version 1\. It's your worst case. Copy it, then cut: fewer locations, fewer shoot days, cheaper format, fewer night scenes.
